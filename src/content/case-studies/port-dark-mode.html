How a gradient and a stroke solved dark mode.

With the light mode UI of Port finalised, it felt complete, except for dark mode. The purple background that looked fresh and lively in light mode simply didn't translate well into a dark theme. 03 / The Challenge

A purple that worked in light mode clashed in dark.

Once the new light mode UI of Port was finalised, it felt complete, except for dark mode. The purple background that looked fresh and lively in light mode simply did not translate well into dark mode. I had to design a solution that:

Maintained brand consistency

All brand colours had to remain the same, with only the primary background open for experimentation.

Spoke to our audience

Port's primary users (17–28 years) value trendy, modern and aesthetic design.

Aligned with Port's brand attributes

Private, techy, modern and Gen Z ready.

And thus started the research.

04 / Research

Grounding the work in dark mode best practices.

I began with dark mode best practices to stay grounded. A few that stood out were:

Avoid pure black. Opt for dark surfaces with subtle colour tones.

Ensure sufficient contrast for readability.

Keep the emotional essence of brand colours without making them harsh on the eyes.

Use accent colours sparingly, as they stand out more in dark mode.

To validate this, I studied apps popular with our target audience: Spotify, Discord, Canva and several on Mobbin. I found three common approaches:

Reducing the opacity of the base colour

Altering the primary colour for dark mode

Using gradients to add depth and richness

Reducing opacity did not give the depth I was looking for, so I decided to try two directions: different shades of our primary purple, and a gradient of the purple. While doing this, I found something else that could work for our dark mode: glassmorphism. Thus began my experimentation.

06 / Outcome

Gradient Variation 2 won.

Gradients and one of the purple shades stood out as the strongest contenders. My design manager preferred the gradient explorations, and we decided to go ahead with Gradient Variation 2, since it offered the most visual richness without hurting readability.

Minor experiments on Gradient Variation 2

Along the way, I played with smaller UI elements, like icons, bottom navigation and input fields, to see if gradients could be extended beyond backgrounds. I highlighted the input fields a little more by giving them a white fill colour, which also went well with the overall aesthetic.
